Top 3: JTBD-03, JTBD-01, JTBD-02

JTBD-03 matters as distinguishing the straightforward cases from the non-straightforward is what will reduce misplaced effort. The reason it matters now, is that the automation will not be useful if it is targeted at the wrong cases, so we first need to identify which cases it applies to. This also has clear business impact, as it answers the question around how much of the workload is viably automatable. It is supported by many pieces of evidence, but primarily by Priya Nair (Operations Manager): "Simple cases that could be resolved in minutes take days because they get stuck in the wrong queue." This influences phase 1 scope by essentially requiring the categorisation system be implemented, not just the automation system. I think this is still reasonable, as categorisation is a pre-requisite for effective self-service, not an added consideration.

JTBD-01 matters as for the self-serve platform to be useful, it needs to meet the basic customer requirements that will satisfy their requests. It matters early on, as designing the platform around the users is the best way to ensure uptake, and remove the burden from the representatives. This evidenced by many observations, but particularly by Dr Lynda Smith (Operations Analyst): "Customers would pay more readily if they understood exactly what they owe and could see options." If customers are more willing to pay, this reduces teh amount of interaction that is needed with them. This doesn't influence phase 1 scope hugely, but it will be important to attempt to quantify the improved experience for customers, and th euptake rate of the self-serve platform.

JTBD-02 matters as it reduces the duplicated effort, and the pain that repeated, redundant communication causes to both the customers and the representatives. That matters now, as it addresses the visibility, customer dissatisfaction and duplication of work themes. This is also supported by many quotes, particularly by Christopher Richards (Collections Representative): "The collections database does not sync with the email tracker, so representatives often re-contact customers who were already promised callbacks."  In terms of phase-1 scope, to address this we need to quantify how much time is spent on duplicated contact tasks
