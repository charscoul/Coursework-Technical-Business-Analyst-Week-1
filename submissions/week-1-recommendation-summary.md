## Recommendation Summary

My strongest recommendation is to focus automation on eligible payment-plan selection. This has the largest ROI, because it not only saves large amounts of agent time, but it helps reduce missed cases and customer re-contact, by making payment plans easier to track and enforce. The model relies on a low-confidence assumption from the finance team, but even conservatively adjusting down the estimates, it is the strongest solution on paper. The autonomy given to customers also gives the soft benefit of greater control and buy-in, as mentioned by some stakeholders. This is also integral to the broader structure of a self-service system, by providing teh basic functionality that constitutes self-service.

In addition to eligible payment-plan selection, digital promise-to-pay capture is a strongly recommended solution. The two have a large interdependency, as capturing digital promises to pay is most effective when integrated with automated payment-plan selection. This ensures that customers are guided through a seamless process, reducing the likelihood of missed payments and improving overall collection efficiency. It also helps to increase enforceability of payment agreements, reducing lost cases. Both would require the creation of new customer interfaces, so aren't necessarily the cheapest solutions, but the freeing of agent time and capturing of missing cases make up for this.

A separate suggestion, that also holds high value, is the portal interaction history for representatives. This reduces the time spent by agents searching for past interactions and ensures they have a complete view of the customer's history, leading to more informed decision-making and improved customer experience. It prevents customers repeating context when transferred, adding soft benefits to their experience, while cutting down on the biggest inefficiencies in the internal process for workers. The reason this solution is not quite as strong as the other two is the high effort and financial requirements. For this to work effectively, the entire internal system would need to be integrated, so that there is no more double-entry and incoherency between data sources. This borders on a full-scale system overhaul, which would be harder to financially recover within a year - though the predictions suggest it is possible. The biggest issue with this solution is concerns of scope, as reworking internal systems is not a part of a self-service platform proposal.
